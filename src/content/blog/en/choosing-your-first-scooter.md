Buying your first electric scooter can feel overwhelming. Specs are confusing, marketing is loud, and "1000W dual motor" doesn't tell you whether it's the right ride for *you*. Here's the short version of what actually matters.

## 1. Range — and the honest kind

Manufacturer range is measured at 20 km/h, on a flat road, with a 75 kg rider and a fresh battery. Real-world range is almost always **60–70% of that number**. If you need 30 km of commuting, look for a scooter rated at 45–50 km.

## 2. Certification

In Germany, only **ABE-certified** scooters can legally ride on public roads. If you plan to commute, certification isn't optional — it's the scooter. Look for the ABE stamp and make sure your insurance sticker is included.

## 3. Weight vs. portability

A 25 kg scooter handles beautifully but you won't want to carry it up stairs. If your commute includes a train or a 4th-floor walkup, keep the weight under 18 kg even if you give up some range.

## 4. Motor power

More watts is not always better. A single 500W motor is plenty for flat cities. Dual-motor 1000W+ shines on hills and off-road — and drains your battery twice as fast.

## 5. Service & parts

The cheapest scooter is the most expensive one when you can't find a replacement controller. Buy from a brand with a real European parts supply chain.
